## Pattern of error

Three patterns visible across the week, all minor but consistent:

1. **Percentage-figure inflation on Google-search / trend stats.** "+2,300% matcha" stands out as the worst offender; "+510% PDRN" verifies cleanly. The routine should treat any "+1,000%" or higher YoY figure as **suspect until the original source URL is in hand**. Cheap fix: require a direct URL in the source citation for every percentage > 200%.

2. **Source-attribution drift on aggregated rankings.** The April 2026 UK TikTok Shop top-5 was attributed to "WWD" when the actual surface is NetInfluencer / FastMoss. WWD is the higher-prestige outlet, which is exactly why the misattribution matters — if a brief cites WWD on stage and a reviewer checks, the credibility hit is disproportionate. Cheap fix: when an aggregator (FastMoss, NetInfluencer, Metricool) carries the data, name the aggregator, not the prestige outlet it cross-cites.

3. **Honorifics + handles on individuals.** Two of two named individuals had small but consequential errors (Niki Ralph mis-titled "Prof"; Lovely Girlie Bits spelled inconsistently across routine files). For outreach-targets specifically, these are first-contact-burning errors. Cheap fix: a 30-second handle/credential verification gate before any name goes into `outreach-seed`.

No structural over-confidence on press-release-sourced sell-out claims (Boots £35 sellout verified across 4+ outlets; the "48h" is the only soft edge). No fabricated dermatologist credentials (Ryan/Ralph both real, correctly affiliated). Brand launches + retail availability are uniformly accurate.
