### H14 — NMN, not raw NAD+, will be the named ingredient creators actually use
**Claim**: NAD+ is hard to stabilize topically (per IPSY / Korean Skincare Coach 2026 sources). NMN (nicotinamide mononucleotide) is the working delivery vehicle. Creators chasing the longevity narrative will increasingly name NMN over NAD+ because NMN-containing products actually deliver visible results. Brands that ride "NAD+" without NMN risk a credibility crash similar to PDRN's "molecules too large" backlash.
**Evidence**: Two fresh 2026 trend sources flag NMN as the stabilized alternative; PDRN's "molecules too large" precedent shows how fast a trust-collapse can happen.
**Confidence**: LOW-MEDIUM. Strong scientific scaffolding, weak creator adoption signal today.
**How to test**: ingredient-mention scan across next 14-day FastMoss pull — track NMN vs NAD+ mention ratio. Watch for a UK/IE dermatologist-creator delivering the "NAD+ molecules don't penetrate" counter-meme.

### H15 — "Korean skincare fatigue" is the meta-narrative; routine-pruning content beats routine-building content
**Claim**: Multiple 2026 trend pieces today name "K-beauty fatigue" / "10-step routines worn people out" / "consumers selective about which trends to adopt." Creators who explicitly prune ("I dropped 4 steps from my routine, here's what I kept") will out-engage creators who add steps. This dovetails with H1 (verdict format) and H11 (James Welsh's restraint moat) — the unified theme is **subtractive content beats additive content**.
**Evidence**: IPSY 2026, Beauty Independent 2026 (slugging/skin-cycling falling off), Korean Skincare Coach 2026 ("less complexity, more clinically substantiated"), Who What Wear 2026 ("3 trends out"). All four 2026 trend pieces consulted today carry the subtractive theme.
**Confidence**: MEDIUM (broad source agreement; no specific creator video yet measured as proof).
**How to test**: caption-mining for "dropped", "stripped", "simplified", "cut" + skincare context across next 14-day FastMoss pool. Compare ER of "added X to my routine" vs "removed X from my routine" framings in the same week.
