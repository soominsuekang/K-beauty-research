## Viral video of the day

**@gracesfaces — "Costco K-beauty store tour", 207 seconds, 653k views, ER 7.76%.**

Why it worked:
1. **Long-form against the short-form rule** — 207s in a 30-60s feed. The audience reads "she actually walked the aisle" as proof of effort. Compression isn't always the trust signal.
2. **Retail as the protagonist, not the product** — the question is "what's on the shelf at Costco?" not "is this product good?" That reframe sidesteps the skepticism filter the audience now applies to product reviews.
3. **Price shock as the hook** — Costco prices vs Sephora prices generates the comparison instinct (H1 verdict mechanic) without naming a loser.

**What we can borrow for IE 18-34**: this video translates directly to a **Sissel Lab Dublin walk-through, an Arnotts K-beauty edit aisle, a Penneys PS… K-Beauty shelf scan, and Henry Street Boots**. Each is a 2-3 minute store tour with the shelf as protagonist, the retailer as the source of trust, and the products as supporting cast. That's the next 30-day IE content franchise nobody owns: **"what's actually on the shelf in [Irish location] this week."**

The runner-up worth studying is **@james_s_welsh's PDRN-debate stance** (29.2k views, ER 4.33%) — "I still use PDRN even though experts still debate amongst themselves whether it…". The structural lesson there is in `james-welsh-model.md` Rule 4 (skepticism acknowledged before endorsement) and Rule 6 (paid content that looks like education) — these are the rules every IE script we commission should pass before going live.

## What this means for IE 18-34 strategy

**Stop briefing routines, start briefing verdicts — and shoot them on real IE shelves.** The convergence across UK FastMoss data and IE comment-section chatter is now too strong to ignore: the audience wants two products compared, one named the loser, and a creator who visibly resists endorsing before endorsing. Every script we commission in the next fortnight should be a head-to-head between two SKUs both buyable in Dublin (Penneys PS… vs Anua, BOJ Relief Sun at Sissel Lab vs Boots IE, Medicube collagen jelly vs BOJ dynasty cream). The retail constraint isn't a limit on the content engine — it **is** the content engine, because it grounds the verdict in a shelf the viewer can walk to. James Welsh's 1.1% paid rate is the discipline target: ~10 of these verdict shoots for every one paid post.

**Recruit Ryan or Ralph at the Institute of Dermatologists for the PDRN piece, before someone else does.** The PDRN credibility war is in its meme-line phase — "molecules too large" is now a verbatim phrase in three UK outlets and is days, not weeks, away from being repeated by the first IE creator. If our first move is a 60-second video shot inside Sissel Lab with one of the Institute's professors naming what she'd actually recommend and what she'd not (Rule 1 of the James Welsh model), we capture the IE PDRN cluster by default — because no one else is in the seat. The opportunity cost of waiting a week is the cluster going to whoever gets there first; the cost of moving now is one outreach email.

**Open the regional sub-franchise immediately.** Every IE K-beauty piece currently defaults to Dublin landmarks (Stephen's Green, Henry Street, Arnotts). The 60-70% of IE 18-34 women who don't live in Greater Dublin are unserved. K-Beauty Cult Galway and Sabrina Hill (Cork) are the levers — a "what's on the K-beauty shelf in Galway/Cork this month" pair with two recurring presenters is cheap to produce, owns an uncontested cluster, and signals to the algorithm that this account is geographically diverse (which lifts FYP distribution).
