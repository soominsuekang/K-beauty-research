### Patterns of error from fact-checks

Three Day-1 framings were caught contradicted in Day 2:

1. **"BOJ has 0 paid UK views in 90d FastMoss pool" framed as anomaly.** Day-2 found the explanation (THG-Commerce GTM via Retail Technology Innovation Hub piece 2026-03-25). The "anomaly" was a deliberate channel choice. **Lesson:** absence of paid views in a category dominated by paid push is not always an anomaly — sometimes it's a brand running an organic-first GTM that AD-tracking can't see by definition. Add to routine: always cross-check "0 views" findings against brand's known GTM channels.

2. **"Boots IE £35 K-Beauty Box sold out" repeated as demand signal.** Day-2 found Boots.ie still lists the box live at €40 "while stock lasts," while UK woman&home reports "sold out fast." Two possibilities: IE has its own SKU or IE restocked. **Lesson:** the sold-out narrative is asymmetric — UK editorial coverage propagates faster than IE retail signal. Always verify the geography of the sellout before repeating the demand framing. Action: physical Dublin Boots check inside 24h.

3. **"Glass skin → bloom skin" handoff framed as clean.** Refinery29 syndicates the bloom-skin succession story; Refinery29 AU updated a separate "Glass Skin Is Trending This Winter" piece 2026-06-05; Runway Magazine reframes glass-skin as "Glass Skin 2.0" (tactile, not wet). UK editorial is internally split. **Lesson:** trend handoffs aren't binary — terms get repositioned, not retired. IE retail copy (Penneys, Stellar.ie) is one full cycle behind UK editorial. Content posture: name the contradiction inside the script rather than pick a winner.
